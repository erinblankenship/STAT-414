Final Project

The purpose of this project is to give you practical experience with the following skills:

  • Formulating research questions that can be answered based on available data, analyzing the data, and interpreting the results

  • Writing a detailed report (in journal article form) that a non-statistician would also understand

  • Presenting your work in way that non-statisticians would understand

  • Working in a group in an efficient way where everyone is involved

The MASDER (Motivational Attitudes in Statistics and Data Science Education Research) project is an NSF grant-funded project that aims to investigate trends and attitudes in statistics and data science education. In the data set provided, the MASDER team was addressing overall questions involving how many schools have data science courses and programs and how many schools have statistics courses and programs.

The MASDER team provided details on their sampling design:

  • Population of interest: US colleges and universities. They excluded non-US schools, service schools, graduate only schools, schools with a total enrollment under 300, some special focus schools. They built their sampling frame using the US Department of Education College Scorecard Database.

  • They used a stratified sample with 8 strata. The strata were based on highest level of degree, selectivity, and minority-serving status:

    • Highest level of degree: Graduate, \(^*\), Bachelors, Associates. \(^*\) means Grad and Bach were combined for MSI due to small stratum size. To define this they used basic Carnegie definitions, then predominant degree offered, then highest degree offered
    • Selectivity: Selective (includes selective and highly selective using the college scorecard), Inclusive, X (Associates)
    • Minority-serving institution: MSI (includes HBCU, HSI, Tribal Colleges), Non-MSI

For this project, your group will:

  • Select at least 5 research questions that can be addressed by the data provided. At least 4 of the research questions must be at the university level and at least 1 must be at the course level.
  • All university-level research questions should be addressed at both the population level and the stratum level.
  • Completely analyze the data to answer the research questions, and be sure to include appropriate measures of variability (SEs/confidence intervals). Do not ignore the fpc.

Deliverables:

  • December 8 & 10: Oral presentation of projects. Each group will have about 15 minutes.

  • December 14: Written report due in Canvas. The report should have the same structure as a scientific publication: abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion/conclusion (no references are required, unless you used some. You may wish to do some additional research into the the US Dept of Ed College Scorecard!).

    • Abstract: This section is typically written after the document is completed. It is basically a paragraph summarizing the motivation for the study being conducted, the research question, the findings, and the conclusion.

    • Introduction: This section introduces the problem and what motivated you to address these specific questions. Basically, tell a story that makes the project interesting to the readers. The introduction section also explains how the paper is organized (what sections and subsections you have and what you include in the sections/subsections).

    • Methods: This section describes the data set and the analysis. This is where you define variables, sampling design, sampling unit, observation unit, and specify how you will be analyzing the data. Basically, if someone wanted to reproduce your results, they could do so based on what is included in this section. Make sure you explain everything so non-statisticians can understand too.

    Results: This section should have tables/plots, and they should be explained thoroughly. Typically, output is not included in the body of the paper itself. Useful parts of the output are summarized in tables and figures and explained in words.

    Discussion: This section concludes why your findings are interesting/important and future avenues for the research. It can also include limitations of the results or any issues you ran into while conducting the analysis.

A few tips:

  • Make sure your text is grammatically correct without typos.
  • Your document should not be longer than 10 pages (including tables and plots).
  • If it helps you with organization, the Introduction, Methods, and Results sections can have subsections.
  • You should include an Appendix with your code (there is no page limit for this part).
  • Since this is a group project is it very important that everyone is involved and works on the project. During the week of December 7, I will provide a questionnaire where you can reflect on your contribution and your teammates’ contributions to the project.
